## Abstract

When an AI shopping agent selects a product, that choice may shape what the platform recommends next. Yet the choice itself may already reflect how the platform arranged the products. We ask whether this influence remains visible after the selected item enters account history. In a research storefront with 460 constructed tasks, two sequential recommenders, and two LLM providers, we trace this process one link at a time. Reordering identical candidate records changed immediate choices. Writing those selections into history then produced small, measurable differences in later shelves and later agent choices for both providers. An extension using ordinary-language shopping tasks also found order-sensitive choices. These findings motivate separating task-local assistance from decisions about what enters account history. The study follows one step of model--recommender feedback; whether it amplifies over repeated interactions remains unresolved.
