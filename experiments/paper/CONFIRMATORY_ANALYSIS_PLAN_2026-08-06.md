# Confirmatory analysis plan: recommendation exposure and behavioral path dependence

Status: statistical core frozen before any DeepSeek call and before any confirmatory-scale call.

## Narrow claim

The paper no longer treats broad “profile pollution” as its central phenomenon. The confirmatory target is narrower: allowing temporary-task activity into a platform recommender can change what an autonomous agent sees, the changed exposure can alter the agent's action path, and later deletion cannot rewind an action that has already changed the resolved history.

“Persistent” and “irreversible” are operational terms here. They mean that paired traces diverge and the protocol-defined next-task ranking remains different after outcome handling because the agent reached a different browsing or resolved-action path. Each arm retains the exclusion mask generated by its realized trace, so this outcome includes downstream candidate-availability changes caused by different browsing. It does not mean that a production model cannot be retrained, that a human preference changed, or that every effect persists indefinitely.

## Evidence boundary

- Observed in the MiMo pilot: immediate activation displaced in-task rankings by 0.6859 on average; 9 of 64 paired immediate-versus-held traces diverged; mean T3 displacement was 0.0422; held and immediate arms had identical aggregate task accuracy and selected utility.
- Observed negative control: logged-but-ranking-inert and no-memory traces matched in 64 of 64 pilot contrasts.
- Inferred mechanism: some post-outcome differences are consistent with recommendation exposure changing the selected action, after which deleting the temporary browsing evidence cannot restore the unobserved held trajectory.
- Not yet supported: recurrence across LLM families, a general model-by-policy interaction, human behavior, or production-retailer persistence.

## Why the pilot rate is not enough

The immediate and held prompts differed because their recommendation slates differed, but API generation can also vary between repeated calls. Content-addressed cache reuse made the earlier inert-log control deterministic and therefore did not estimate independent-call stochastic divergence. The confirmatory design adds two independently generated held-exposure sham trajectories. The central quantity is the excess persistent-feedback rate above this sham divergence, not the raw 9/64 rate.

## Primary estimand

For each task and cell:

`persistent behavioral feedback = 1(trace differs AND next-task Top-10 displacement > 0)`.

The exposure event compares immediate activation with held activation. The sham event compares two independent held-activation trajectories with identical semantic inputs. The cell-level adjusted effect is the exposure event minus the sham event.

For each LLM family, the primary task-level estimand averages this adjusted effect over the frozen 2 × 2 grid of recommender families and agent policies. Categories receive equal weight. This makes the two LLM-family replication decisions primary; individual recommender-by-policy cells are secondary heterogeneity analyses.

## Sample size

The MiMo pilot's largest cell SD was 0.478714. The frozen precision rule implies 353 valid tasks per cell for a 0.05 normal-approximation half-width. The main study therefore plans 360 tasks—90 per category—fully crossed through both LLMs, both sequential recommenders, and both agent policies. This yields at least 353 attempted observations per cell while preserving category balance.

This is a precision-based pilot rule, not a post hoc power claim. Before the main run, the analysis script must reproduce the rule mechanically and report the detectable adjusted risk difference under the observed sham rate.

## Tests and multiplicity

Each LLM family receives one primary replication test. A 10,000-replicate category-stratified task bootstrap estimates its adjusted effect. Because two independent families must replicate, each family must have a Bonferroni-adjusted one-sided 97.5% lower bound above zero. A 100,000-draw paired task-level sign-flip test is the confirmatory randomization check.

The eight LLM × recommender × policy estimates are secondary. Their one-sided paired permutation p-values are Holm-adjusted within LLM family; all point estimates and intervals are reported regardless of significance.

## Guardrails

- Manipulation: T1 displacement lower 95% bound above 0.15 in each LLM family.
- Stochastic calibration: sham persistent-feedback upper one-sided 95% bound at most 0.05.
- Reliability: at least 95% valid structured actions and below 5% timeout rate per family.
- Current-task preservation: paired TOST for held-minus-immediate decision accuracy and normalized selected utility, both with ±0.05 equivalence margins.
- Missingness: no task replacement after outcomes; complete-pair analysis plus a worst-case sensitivity that penalizes missing exposure arms and favors missing sham arms.

## Decision outcomes

- **Cross-family recurrence:** both LLM-family primary tests and all reliability, sham, and task-local guardrails pass.
- **Model-contingent feedback:** only one LLM family passes or the adjusted effects are materially heterogeneous.
- **Ranking mechanism only:** exposure changes T1 slates but adjusted persistent feedback is not positive; remove the irreversible-behavior contribution.
- **No-go/redesign:** sham divergence or API failure exceeds its gate, or the worst-case sensitivity removes the effect.
