## Title

From Shelf Order to Later Agent Choice: A Link-by-Link Audit of Recommendation Writeback

## Abstract (135 words)

When an AI shopping agent uses a personalized platform, the platform orders
what it sees and may learn from what it selects. We test whether an order-only
difference remains measurable after this cycle. In a research storefront with
460 pseudonymous tasks, two sequential recommenders, and two LLM providers,
activating temporary task evidence changed the current Top-10. Reordering
identical candidate records also changed categorical selections. After
standardized writeback, later shelves and model outputs differed for DeepSeek
(0.035 [0.017, 0.054]; 34/1,840 matched comparisons) and MiMo (0.017 [0.005,
0.032]; 24/1,840); byte-identical controls were zero. Both providers also
remained order-sensitive in a 64-task product-language extension. A single
matched random-writeback comparison did not establish equivalence or rule out
amplification. The study measures one-step model behavior in a research
storefront. Human behavior, production effects, and writeback policies remain
outside its scope.

## Short contribution statement

The paper follows one order-only exposure change through agent selection,
standardized writeback to a frozen sequential recommender, and one later model
selection. The final endpoint recurs in two LLM providers. A product-language
extension measures task outcomes and compares multistep propagation with one
random realization matched on write count and paired-write equality. That
comparison cannot establish equivalence or selection-specific amplification.
We did not evaluate the proposed outcome gate.
